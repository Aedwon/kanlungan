# Makers

Status: **Decided on delegation.** All makers, artists, and their stories are invented. The names have not been checked against real businesses. See `docs/decisions.md`.

Eight Philippine makers and two international makers. This ratio follows the Philippine-first rule in `docs/concept/shop.md`.

## Kanlungan Originals

Not a maker page. Pieces designed by the studio and built by partner workshops in Pampanga and Cebu. Attribution on products reads **Kanlungan Original**.

## Philippine makers

### Lilok Workshop

- **Place:** Betis, Pampanga
- **Craft:** Solid hardwood furniture and carving
- **Story:** A family workshop in a town known for woodcarving. Three generations have worked at the same benches. Lilok builds in plantation mahogany and acacia with hand-cut joinery and oil finishes. The workshop makes a small number of pieces at a time and signs each one underneath.

### Sulihiya Studio

- **Place:** Cebu City, Cebu
- **Craft:** Rattan and cane seating and objects
- **Story:** A studio of weavers and frame makers who work with rattan poles and hand-woven cane. Sulihiya keeps the open, breathable weave that suits a warm climate and pairs it with clean, modern frames. Each chair takes about four days to weave.

### Tikog Loom

- **Place:** Basey, Samar
- **Craft:** Handwoven mats, rugs, and wall pieces in tikog reed and abaca
- **Story:** A weaving cooperative of about forty women. They harvest, dry, and flatten tikog reed, then weave it by hand on the floor. Kanlungan works with the cooperative on larger sizes and quieter, undyed patterns.

### Bukal Clayworks

- **Place:** Tiwi, Albay
- **Craft:** Stoneware vessels and lamp bases
- **Story:** A small pottery that uses local clay and wood-ash glazes. The pieces are thrown by hand and fired in small batches, so no two glazes match exactly. Bukal makes vessels meant to be used, not only displayed.

### Habol Textiles

- **Place:** Miagao, Iloilo
- **Craft:** Handloomed cotton cushions and throws
- **Story:** A group of handloom weavers who work in cotton with a dense, soft hand. Habol weaves short runs in a narrow range of natural and plant-dyed colors. The cloth softens with each wash.

### Tanso Lighting Works

- **Place:** Marikina, Metro Manila
- **Craft:** Brass, glass, and capiz shell lighting
- **Story:** A metal workshop that spins and finishes brass by hand and pairs it with opal glass and capiz shell. Tanso leaves its brass unlacquered so it darkens slowly with age. Every fitting is wired and tested in the workshop. The workshop also makes small metal tables.

### Marmol Atelier

- **Place:** Romblon, Romblon
- **Craft:** Stone tables and carved objects
- **Story:** A stone workshop on an island known for its marble. The atelier cuts tabletops and carves small objects from local stone in gray, cream, and soft green. Finishes are honed, not polished, so the stone feels soft to the touch. Marble offcuts are cast into terrazzo for tables.

### Amihan Editions

- **Place:** Quezon City, Metro Manila
- **Craft:** Limited-edition prints and original paintings
- **Story:** A print studio and small gallery that publishes work by Philippine artists. Amihan prints on cotton rag paper in editions of fifty or fewer. Artists represented in the shop: **Ines Dalisay** (abstract works on paper) and **Paolo Sarmiento** (landscape paintings).

## International makers

### Okabe Mokko

- **Place:** Nagano, Japan
- **Craft:** Small furniture in hinoki cypress
- **Story:** A two-person woodworking shop that makes stools and small tables from Japanese cypress. The pale wood and precise joinery sit well beside Philippine hardwoods. Kanlungan carries one of their pieces.

### Forma Lenta

- **Place:** Caldas da Rainha, Portugal
- **Craft:** Ceramic pendant lights
- **Story:** A ceramics studio that slip-casts lampshades in matte, unglazed stoneware. The shades give a warm, even light that Kanlungan uses over dining tables. The studio produces to order in small batches.
