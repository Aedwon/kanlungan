**Eight conflicts to note.**

1. **The taro in a dark jar.** The picked image keeps it. Lilim has giant taro in black jars, and the register forbids the repeat. No exception is argued: a second taro jar is exactly what Aedwon caught in round 4. The villa keeps what the taro did, which is one large, dark, living thing with big leaves on the right side of the room. It is now a bird's nest fern in a tall cylinder planter of dark woven nito vine. The fern grows on the coconut trunks of the grove, so the gardeners bring one in for each villa. It is not a palm and it has no flowers. Pending review.
2. **The bedside lamps.** The picked image has green glazed jar lamps with linen shades. That is the shape of the Lilim living room lamp. The villas get a pair of box lanterns. Moss green stays in the cushions.
3. **The painting.** The picked image shows a landscape of trees, which pictures the grove. Tanaw also has a landscape painting. The villas get still lifes.
4. **The hero camera.** The approved before uses the round 4 recipe, as the Tanaw and Dalampasigan heroes do: a view down the room to the opening, with a door on the right. An approved before is never regenerated, so it stays. The frames differ in what they show: a four-post bed, a wall of sliding doors, and a grove. The terrace pair can lead the page if Aedwon wants the five heroes to differ more.
5. **The four-post frame and the fan.** The studio hand's general rule says that no bed frame stands under a ceiling fan. Its Pahinga section keeps the four-post bed and asks for both heights and a frame clear of the blades. The specific instruction is followed. The fan is in the middle of the room, and a bed with its head on the left wall reaches under the edge of its sweep wherever it stands, unless it blocks the sliding doors. The frame is 1.90 m tall and the blades turn at about 2.35 m, so 45 cm is clear. This is an exception to the general rule, and Aedwon decides whether it stands. See section 5.
6. **The old chest.** The Lilim main bedroom has the family's old chest at the foot of the bed. The villa chest is kept, because Aedwon's keep list names it. It stands against a wall under a painting, and each villa has a different one.
7. **The cane headboard.** Lilim and Dalampasigan each have a cane headboard. It is in the picked image and it is kept. Pending review.
8. **The side tables.** The Lilim main bedroom also has two ebonized Sulok Side Tables. The Sulok is on no other project's shop list, so Pahinga is its project.

## 2. Brief

A couple from Cebu built eight villas and a reception pavilion in a family coconut grove, a short walk from the beach. The builder handed the buildings over white, tiled, and empty.

Their guests are couples who stay three to five nights. They sleep late, eat breakfast on the terrace, walk to the beach, and come back in the heat of the afternoon. A staff of seven runs the property.

What each villa has: a television, a router, a kettle with cups, a water bottle and glasses, a flashlight, an umbrella, two beach towels, and a key.

What they asked:

- Each villa should feel like a private house, not a hotel room.
- The villas must stay easy to run: pieces that can be repaired, and one stock of spares for all eight.
- A television in each villa, because guests stay several nights and it rains. It should not face the bed.
- Breakfast is carried to each terrace, so the terrace needs a table for two.
- Guests check in sitting down.
- Nothing built. The owners had just finished paying the builder.

Kanlungan took the property from signed proposal to opening in 20 weeks, with 8 days on site.

## 3. Concept

**A plain white villa, furnished like the old house of someone with taste: one mahogany bed under white netting, cloth in tobacco, rust, and moss, one old chest and two paintings that belong to that villa alone, and a terrace that is a second room.**

Mood: shaded, unhurried, collected.

This is the picked image with a design behind it. It keeps what Aedwon chose: a mahogany four-post bed with netting, oat linen, moss and rust textiles, a striped rug, tobacco curtains, an old chest, a painting, one large plant, and white walls that stay white. It corrects what the studio hand names: the sockets, the frame and the fan, and the painting's subject. It adds what the studio hand asks for: the rest of the room, the screen, the bath, the terrace, the pavilion, and rooms that are lit in the evening.

For a villa, "lived in" means hosted. A villa is ready for a guest, or a guest has just put a bag down. It is never cluttered.

The studio hand changes one line of the old copy: "very little else" becomes "few things, all of them complete."

| Room | Leading piece |

### The moves

1. **One bed leads every villa.** A mahogany four-post frame with netting, made eight times.
2. **Each villa has three things of its own.** One old chest and two paintings. No two villas share them. Everything else is the same in all eight and comes from one stock.
3. **Cloth carries the color.** Tobacco at the doors, rust and moss on the bed and the chairs, and tobacco stripes on the floor. The walls stay white.
4. **The room has two halves.** The bed is near the doors and the grove. The settee and the screen are near the entry, where the screen does not face the bed.
5. **The terrace is a room.** A daybed, a table for two, and a lamp over the table, all in front of the bath wall. The slab in front of the sliding doors stays clear, so the bed looks straight into the grove.
6. **Guests check in at a table, sitting down.** The pavilion has no counter.

One line on the page states that nothing was built or painted.

### How Pahinga differs from the other four

It is the second hospitality project, and it is the darkest and warmest in material: red-brown mahogany, tobacco, rust, and moss against new white walls. It is the only project with a four-post bed, the only one with still life paintings, and the only one led by cloth color. Dalampasigan is honey wood, white linen, and no color. Its register row lists what no other project may repeat.

| Color | Tobacco, rust, and moss, all in cloth. The green of the fern. Outside, the grove. | Curtains, throw, cushions, rug stripes |

Rules for this project:

- One bold thing per room at most. Bedroom: the striped rug. Sitting corner: the painting. Terrace: none, the grove is enough. Pavilion: the large painting.
- No furniture matches the cream floor.
- Color comes from cloth and paint on canvas. No colored furniture, no colored wall.
- No stone table, no brass lamp, no capiz, no reed, no acacia in honey finish, and no indigo.
- Something on a wall in every room.

No material board is made unless pieces drift between frames.
