## 2. Brief

The owner is a book editor in her early thirties. This is the first home she has owned. She had a budget to spread over a year and wanted a plan to buy toward, starting with a few good pieces.

She works at home two days a week, at the dining table. She reads on the sofa. She cooks most nights and wanted to grow what she cooks with. Friends come for dinner about once a month.

What she owns: about six hundred books, her grandmother's cane lounge chair, a laptop, a small speaker, a rice cooker, a kettle, and a refrigerator.

She owns no television and does not want one. She watches on the laptop, on the sofa or in bed. The design book keeps the console wall and its socket free for a screen if she changes her mind.

What she asked:

- A plan she can carry out herself, in stages, with nothing bought twice.
- Keep the parquet as it is.
- Somewhere for the books.
- A balcony she can garden on.
- One strong move she can do with her own hands.

Kanlungan delivered the design book in 7 weeks: a layout, a palette, renders of the living room and the bedroom, and a sourcing list in three stages. She did the rest in 12 months.

| Stage | Months | What she did |
|---|---|---|
| One | 1 to 2 | Painted the long wall. Bought the sofa, the rug, the floor lamp, and the three paper shades. Changed the bulbs. |
| Two | 4 to 6 | Bought the bed, the bedside table, the console, and the curtains. Framed the prints. |
| Three | 9 to 12 | Planted the balcony. Bought the stools, the bookcase, the dining table, and the chairs, and painted the chairs. |

## 3. Concept

**An old apartment kept as it is, with one long wall painted and a few good pieces bought one at a time, so that after a year it holds her books, her grandmother's chair, and a garden.**

Mood: small, personal, grown.

This is the picked image with a design behind it. It keeps what Aedwon chose: a clay two-seat sofa, an oat rug over old parquet, the inherited cane chair, books in stacks, plants, a slim black console, and two lamps lit on a gray afternoon. It corrects what the studio hand names: the room is lit as lived. It adds what the studio hand asks for: the rest of the home, a decision on the screen, and the one painted wall.

Hardin is photographed a year on. It is the most lived-in of the five projects. It is still edited: each thing has a place.

| Room | Leading piece |

| Dining end | The scrubbed table that is also her desk |
| Bedroom | The bed, in oat linen |

### The moves

1. **One wall is painted.** The long north wall of the living and dining room, from the entry to the window. Every other wall and every ceiling stays gray-white. She painted it herself in a weekend.
2. **The parquet is left alone.** Wear is its character. A wool rug holds the seats.
3. **A few good pieces, and the rest found.** The sofa, the bed, the rug, and the console are from the shop. The tables, the chairs, and the bookcase are secondhand.
4. **Her things are the decoration.** The books, the cane chair, and the prints she framed.
5. **The balcony is a kitchen garden.** What grows there is cooked in the kitchen.
6. **Lamps in place of bare bulbs.** A paper shade at each ceiling point and a lamp at each seat.

One line on the page states that the owner did the work, and that nothing was built.

### How Hardin differs from the other four

It is the only project the studio did not furnish, the only one with a painted wall, the only one with a worn floor, and the only one shown a year on. It is the smallest and the fullest. Tanaw is also a Makati condo, and it is new, white, high, and lit at dusk around a screen. Hardin is old, low, gray-lit, and has no screen.

### The painted wall

**Picked by Aedwon (2026-11-16): A, a muted gray-green, like dried sage.** Matte. Light to middle in tone and low in strength, about `#98A28E`. It sits opposite the red-brown floor and the clay sofa in color, so both look richer against it. It reads as paint, not as a color cast. It is quiet enough for the bedroom door to stand open onto it.

| Option | Color | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **A, working choice** | Gray-green, dried sage, about `#98A28E` | It is the expected partner for clay and plants. It could read as safe. |
| B | Gray-blue, an overcast sky, about `#93A1AB` | Cooler and calmer. On a gray day the room may read cold, and it does less for the floor. |
| C | Pale clay plaster, a muted pink-beige, about `#C9A592` | The warmest and most tonal. The sofa loses its outline against it, and it can be mistaken for a warm cast. |

Aedwon picks from rendered images. The optional job in the gate file renders the hero in B and C. If he picks B or C, four more frames change: the render, the dining frame, the design book, and the moodboard.

The paint stops at the ceiling, at the baseboard, and at each corner. The air conditioner, the sockets, and the baseboard stay white.

Rules for this project:

- One bold thing per room at most. Living room: the wall. Bedroom: none. Balcony: none.
- No furniture matches the red-brown floor.
- No other wall is painted. No brass, no capiz, no reed, no rattan, no mahogany in natural finish, no striped rug, and no moss or tobacco cloth.
- Something on a wall in every room.
