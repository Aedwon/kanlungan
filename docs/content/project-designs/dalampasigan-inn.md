## 2. Brief

Two siblings took over an older inn near the surf beach. They cleared and repainted it themselves. They needed twelve rooms and two shared rooms ready for guests before the surf season, about one season away.

Their guests are surfers and weekend visitors from Manila. Most are out before sunrise, come back salty and sandy, eat breakfast together, and are out again until dark. A staff of five prepares the rooms.

What the inn owns: twelve room keys, a guest register, a card terminal, a router, a printer, the kitchen and its appliances, one television, a shelf of paperbacks that guests have left behind, and a few board games.

What they asked:

- One room that a small staff can prepare the same way twelve times.
- Pieces that take salt air, sand, and wet towels, and that can be repaired.
- No television in the guest rooms. The rooms face the sea or the garden, guests are out all day, and the wifi is good. The inn's one screen is in the breakfast room, for the evenings.
- A lobby and a breakfast room that feel open after dark, when guests come back.
- Nothing built. The owners had just paid for the repainting.

Kanlungan took the inn from signed proposal to opening in 16 weeks, with 9 days on site.

## 3. Concept

**A plain inn made ready: white linen, honey wood, and reed in every room, the sea left as the only color wherever there is sea, and one long table downstairs where the whole inn meets.**

Mood: plain, ready, hosted.

This is the picked image with a design behind it. It keeps what Aedwon chose: white walls and linen, honey acacia, reed, one thin black line, and the sea as the only color. It corrects what he named: the sockets and the curtain. It adds what the studio hand asks for: a lamp on each side of the bed, the rest of the inn, and rooms downstairs that are fuller and lit at night.

For an inn, "lived in" means hosted. A room is ready for a guest, or a guest has just put a bag down. It is never cluttered.

The guest rooms are the quietest rooms. The lobby and the breakfast room are fuller: more wood, more things in use, and lamps on in the evening.

### The moves

1. **One room, made twelve times.** The same pieces in the same places in every room. Staff can prepare any room without thinking.
2. **Where there is sea, no color. Where there is none, the sea comes in as cloth.** The eight sea-side rooms have undyed cotton. The four garden-side rooms have the same throw and cushion in indigo.
3. **Everything takes sand and salt.** Solid oiled acacia that can be sanded and oiled again. Reed mats, not wool. Cotton and linen that go in the wash. No lacquer and no upholstery.
4. **The ceiling is left to the fans.** The tubes got soft white tubes. Every lamp Kanlungan added stands on something. Nothing hangs.
5. **One table for the whole inn.** Guests sit together or apart at one long table, with benches on one side and chairs on the other.
6. **One artist, one drawing for each room.**

One line on the page states that nothing was built or painted.

### How Dalampasigan differs from the other four

It is the hospitality project and the plainest shell. It is the only project that repeats one room, the only one led by honey acacia and reed, and the only one with no pendant, no brass lamp, and no upholstered seat. Most of its frames are in morning light. Its register row lists what no other project may repeat.

## 6. Palette and materials

Designed for this inn: a plain white shell with cream tile, hard coastal light, and salt in the air. The view supplies the color upstairs. Wood and lamp light supply the warmth downstairs.

| Role | What | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Pale | White walls (the owners'). White linen and cotton. Cream tile (the owners'). Undyed cotton. | Curtains, bed linen, towels, throw, lamp shade in the lobby |
| Dark | Thin blackened steel. The screen. Graphite lines. | Clothes rail, picture frames, the floor lamp's foot, the trestle pins |
| Warm | Oiled acacia, honey in tone, in every room. Natural cane. Tikog reed. Bamboo. Rattan binding. | Bed, stools, benches, desk, long table, chairs, serving table, mats, lamps |
| Color | The sea, in the sea-side rooms. Indigo, in the garden-side rooms. Downstairs, small doses: a thin indigo stripe on the napkins, the green and yellow of fruit, the green of the calamansi tree. | Window, throw and cushion, table |

Rules for this project:

- One bold thing per room at most. Sea-side room: none, the window is enough. Garden-side room: the indigo throw. Lobby: the calamansi tree. Breakfast room: the fruit on the table.
- No furniture matches the cream floor.
- Rattan appears only as binding and as trays. There is no rattan furniture.
- No stone, no brass lamp, no glass lamp, no paper shade, and no capiz.
- Something on a wall in every room.

No material board is made unless pieces drift between frames.
