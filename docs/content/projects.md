## Lilim House

Revised on 2026-11-16. The design is in [the Lilim design file](project-designs/lilim-house.md). The captions are provisional until the photographs are approved. Field names and the caption format stay as the site expects them.

- **Location:** Antipolo, Rizal
- **Type:** Residential, family house
- **Floor area:** 320 sqm
- **Service:** Full-Service Furnishing
- **Year:** 2025
- **Scope:** Living, dining, kitchen, family room, three bedrooms, covered terrace
- **Duration:** 18 weeks from signed proposal to handover, with 6 days on site
- **As handed over:** A new house, finished by the contractor and empty. White painted walls, pale gray tile, flat white ceilings with recessed downlights, bronze aluminum sliding doors, and a bare bulb at each ceiling point.
- **Brief:** A family of five moved into the first house they built, on a hillside above a green valley. The contractor handed it over white, tiled, and empty. They wanted it calm and finished from the first night, with the view left open and nothing more built.
- **Approach:** The studio kept the great room quiet, so the valley is the color. Charcoal linen, black wood, and steel sit against white linen, pale wool, and gray stone, with cane and warm wood in every room. One slab of Romblon marble leads the living room. The family's books and screen fill a room of their own. Nothing was built or painted.
- **Material palette:** Honed Romblon marble, ebonized plantation mahogany, blackened steel, white and charcoal linen, wool, cane
- **Shop the project:** Silong Sofa (charcoal), Dapithapon Lounge Chair, Lambot Wool Rug, Luad Table Lamp, Sinag Floor Lamp, Hapag Dining Table (ebonized mahogany), Hain Dining Chair (ebonized mahogany), Kapis Pendant, Tapayan Vessel, Yakap Sofa (sand), Baul Credenza (ebonized), Lenta Pendant, Himlay Bed (ebonized mahogany), Aparador Cabinet, Duyan Daybed, Batis Coffee Table
- **Image notes:** A white, shaded living room open to a green valley, with a charcoal sofa, a large stone table, white linen curtains, and lamps lit in the late afternoon

### Approach, expanded

The house looks over a valley, and green fills every opening. We kept the great room quiet, so the valley is the color. Charcoal linen, black wood, and steel sit against white linen, pale wool, and gray Romblon marble. One stone table, made for the house, leads the living room. Cane and warm wood appear in every room.

The builder left cool ceiling lights. We changed every bulb to a warm one and added lamps where the family sits, eats, and reads. White linen runs from the ceiling for the full length of the glass. We left the middle of the great room open, because three children need a floor. Their books, their games, and the screen have a room of their own. Nothing was built or painted.

- **Location:** Bonifacio Global City, Taguig
- **Type:** Residential, two-bedroom condo
- **Floor area:** 118 sqm
- **Service:** Full-Service Furnishing
- **Year:** 2025
- **Scope:** Living, dining and kitchen, main bedroom, main bath, study and guest room, entry
- **Duration:** 14 weeks from signed proposal to handover, with 4 days on site
- **As handed over:** A bare unit from the developer, clean and empty. White painted walls, wood-look vinyl floors, flat ceilings with recessed downlights, fixed glass facing west, the developer's kitchen and wardrobes, and a bare bulb at each ceiling point.

One long console, made for the unit, holds the records, the turntable, and the screen. Every seat has a lamp, so the rooms stay lit against the city at dusk. Nothing was built or painted.

### Approach, expanded

The unit is a white box on a high floor, with towers outside and no green. We let the couple's own things warm it. Both owners come home late. The developer left cool ceiling lights. We changed every bulb and put a lamp at every seat, so the rooms are lit when the city turns blue. Oat linen runs from the ceiling across each wall of glass. The second bedroom works as a study and takes a guest. The kitchen, the wardrobes, and the floor are the developer's. ## Dalampasigan Inn

Revised on 2026-11-16. The design is in [the Dalampasigan design file](project-designs/dalampasigan-inn.md). The captions are provisional until the photographs are approved. Field names and the caption format stay as the site expects them.

- **Location:** San Juan, La Union
- **Type:** Hospitality, 12-room boutique inn
- **Floor area:** 640 sqm
- **Service:** Full-Service Furnishing
- **Year:** 2024
- **Scope:** Twelve guest rooms and baths, lobby, breakfast room, corridors
- **Duration:** 16 weeks from signed proposal to opening, with 9 days on site
- **As handed over:** An older inn, cleared and repainted by its new owners, and empty. White painted walls, cream floor tile, aluminum sliding windows, and a tube light, a ceiling fan, and an air conditioner in each room.
- **Brief:** Two siblings took over an older inn near the surf beach. They needed twelve rooms and two shared rooms ready for guests in one season, with pieces that survive salt air and sand, and rooms that a small staff can prepare the same way every day.
- **Approach:** The studio designed one room and made it twelve times. Rooms that face the sea have no color but the sea. Rooms that face the garden have the same cloth in indigo. Furniture is natural oiled acacia, honey in tone, solid and repairable. Reed mats replace wool rugs because they handle sand. The breakfast room has one long table, made for the room, for the whole inn. Nothing was built or painted.
- **Material palette:** Natural oiled acacia (honey), cane, tikog reed, white linen, handloomed cotton, stoneware, blackened steel
- **Shop the project:** Himlay Bed (acacia), Tuntungan Stool, Bangko Bench, Banig Rug, Kumot Throw, Hain Dining Chair (acacia), Bilao Tray, Hibla Wall Piece
- **Image notes:** A plain white guest room in bright morning light, with a honey acacia bed with a cane headboard, white linen, a reed mat, and the sea through one window

3. Guest room. A room that faces the garden, with the same layout and the Kumot Throw in indigo.

9. Breakfast room. In the evening the far end is the common corner, with the inn's one screen.

### Approach, expanded

The owners needed twelve rooms ready in one season. We designed one room and made it twelve times. Eight rooms face the sea, and in those the sea is the only color. Four face the garden, and in those the throw and one cushion are indigo. Every room has the same pieces in the same places, so the staff can prepare any of them the same way. Each room has one drawing, and no two are alike.

Salt, sand, and heavy use shape every choice. The Himlay and the Hain share the honey tone of natural oiled acacia. Furniture is solid wood that can be sanded, oiled, and repaired. Tikog reed mats clean easily. The ceilings belong to the fans, so every lamp we added stands on something. In the breakfast room, one long table lets guests sit together or apart. The walls, the tile, the windows, the baths, and the kitchen are the owners'. Nothing was built or painted.

## Pahinga Villas

Revised on 2026-11-16. The design is in [the Pahinga design file](project-designs/pahinga-villas.md). The captions are provisional until the photographs are approved. Field names and the caption format stay as the site expects them.

- **Location:** Panglao, Bohol
- **Type:** Hospitality, 8-villa resort
- **Floor area:** 520 sqm
- **Service:** Full-Service Furnishing
- **Year:** 2025
- **Scope:** Eight one-bedroom villas with baths and terraces, reception pavilion
- **Duration:** 20 weeks from signed proposal to opening, with 8 days on site
- **As handed over:** Eight new villas and a pavilion, finished by the builder and empty. White painted walls, cream floor tile, flat white ceilings with a fan and recessed downlights, aluminum sliding doors, and a bare concrete terrace in front of each villa.
- **Brief:** The owners built eight villas in a coconut grove a short walk from the beach. They wanted each villa to feel like a private house, not a hotel room, and to stay easy to maintain between guests, with nothing more built.
- **Approach:** Each villa has few things, all of them complete. A four-post bed in natural mahogany, made for the villas, stands under white netting and faces the grove. Cloth carries the color: tobacco linen at the doors, rust and moss on the bed, and a striped abaca rug. A settee and the screen fill the other half of the room. Each villa has one old chest and two paintings that no other villa has. Everything else comes from one stock, so any piece can be repaired or replaced. Nothing was built or painted.
- **Material palette:** Natural plantation mahogany, cane and rattan, abaca, tobacco linen, oat linen, handloomed cotton in rust and moss, ebonized acacia, nito vine, stoneware
- **Shop the project:** Dapithapon Lounge Chair, Sulok Side Table (ebonized), Bakal Side Table, Hablon Cushion (moss, rust), Kumot Throw (rust), Aparador Cabinet
- **Image notes:** A white villa bedroom in late morning light, with a mahogany four-post bed under white netting, tobacco curtains, a striped rug, rust and moss cloth, an old chest, and a coconut grove through open sliding doors

### Approach, expanded

The owners wanted each villa to feel like a private house. We gave each one few things, all of them complete. A four-post bed in natural mahogany stands under white netting, with its foot toward the sliding doors and the grove. A cane settee and the screen fill the other half of the room, so the screen does not face the bed. The terrace is a second room, with a daybed and a table for two. Breakfast is carried there.

The owners also need the villas to stay easy to run. The bed, the settee, the tables, the lamps, and the cloth are the same in all eight villas, so staff can repair or replace any piece from one stock. Three things differ: each villa has its own old chest and its own two paintings. Cloth carries the color, in tobacco, rust, and moss. The walls, the tile, the sliding doors, the baths, and the terrace slabs are the owners'. Nothing was built or painted.

## Hardin Apartment

- **Location:** Legazpi Village, Makati
- **Type:** Residential, one-bedroom condo
- **Floor area:** 74 sqm
- **Service:** Interior Design Direction
- **Year:** 2026
- **Scope:** Full unit design book, renders of living room and bedroom
- **Duration:** 7 weeks to the design book. The owner then furnished the apartment in three stages over 12 months.
- **As handed over:** An older unit, emptied. Gray-white painted walls, old hardwood parquet, worn and left as found, aluminum windows, one bare bulb in each room, and a narrow balcony facing east. The kitchen, the bath, and the bedroom closet are the building's.
- **Brief:** A first-time owner wanted a clear plan to buy toward over a year, starting with a few good pieces.
- **Approach:** The studio delivered a layout, a palette, and a sourcing list in three stages. The owner did the work herself. She painted one long wall gray-green and kept the old parquet as it was. Stage one was the wall, the clay Silong Sofa, a rug, and a lamp. Stage two added the oat Unan Bed, the Bakal Side Table, the ebonized Pasilyo, and the curtains. Stage three was the balcony garden, the dining table, and the bookcase, all found secondhand. Her books, her grandmother's cane chair, and the prints she framed do the rest. The photographs show the apartment a year on. Nothing was built.
- **Material palette:** Old hardwood parquet, one gray-green painted wall, clay and oat linen, oat wool, ebonized plantation mahogany, cane, hinoki, blackened steel, clay pots
- **Shop the project:** Silong Sofa (two-seat, clay), Lambot Wool Rug (170 × 240), Pasilyo Console Table (ebonized mahogany), Unan Bed (queen, oat), Bakal Side Table, Asa Stool, Tabing Linen Curtain (oat), Amihan No. 3, Bato Object
- **Image notes:** A narrow living room in an older apartment on a gray afternoon, a year after the owner moved in. One long wall painted gray-green, a clay two-seat sofa, an oat rug on worn parquet, an inherited cane chair, books in stacks, and two lamps lit. Shown beside the studio's render, the design book, a moodboard, and the floor plan. A small balcony garden of herbs.

### Approach, expanded

The owner was buying a first home and had a budget to spread over a year. We designed the whole unit at once, so every later purchase fits the plan. The design book holds the layout, the palette, renders of the living room and the bedroom, and a specification for each room. She did the work herself.

The sourcing list has three stages. Stage one is the long wall, which she painted in a weekend, and the sofa, a rug, and a lamp, which make the living room usable at once. Stage two adds the oat Unan Bed, the Bakal Side Table, the ebonized Pasilyo console, and the curtains. Stage three is the balcony garden and the pieces she found secondhand: a dining table she scrubbed and oiled, four chairs she painted black, and a bookcase. The old parquet stays as it was, and so do the building's kitchen, bath, and closet. Her books and her grandmother's cane chair were always part of the plan. She bought when ready, and nothing needed replacing along the way.
